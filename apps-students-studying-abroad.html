Life abroad, made easier

Apps for Students Studying Abroad

Because studying abroad is more than traveling. It's learning how to live somewhere new, build a routine, and turn unfamiliar places into a second home.

Feel like a local, even when you're far from home.

From grocery shopping and managing expenses to making new friends and learning another language, everyday life abroad brings its own challenges. The right digital tools can help students adjust more comfortably and independently.

These apps are useful for building a routine, staying connected, and making student life in Europe more manageable. Availability, pricing, and features may vary by location.

LANGUAGE LEARNING

Duolingo

Practice vocabulary and basic language skills through short, interactive lessons. A useful way to build confidence before and during your semester abroad.

Student tip: Learn phrases for ordering food, asking directions, and introducing yourself.

Visit Duolingo →
EVERYDAY COMMUNICATION

WhatsApp

Stay connected with classmates, roommates, local friends, and family through messages, group chats, and internet-based calls.

Student tip: Create group chats for your classes, apartment, and weekend travel plans.

Visit WhatsApp →
BUDGETING

Splitwise

Keep track of shared expenses with roommates and friends, including groceries, meals, and group travel costs.

Student tip: Record expenses as they happen so group spending stays organized.

Visit Splitwise →
CURRENCY & PAYMENTS

Wise

Explore international currency conversion and transfer services in eligible locations, with fee information available before transactions.

Student tip: Compare exchange costs with your existing bank and confirm account eligibility.

Visit Wise →
FOOD SAVINGS

Too Good To Go

Find participating businesses offering surplus food at reduced prices through the app, depending on availability.

Student tip: Look for participating bakeries and cafes near your university or apartment.

Visit Too Good To Go →
FOOD DELIVERY

LOCAL DINING

OpenTable

Discover participating restaurants, explore dining options, and make reservations where the service is available.

Student tip: Research local restaurants for birthdays, group dinners, and special occasions.

NAVIGATION

Google Maps

Navigate your host city, discover nearby businesses, and save frequently visited locations such as your university, apartment, and favorite cafe.

Student tip: Save your home address and key campus locations before your first day of classes.

A little guidance goes a long way

Your first week abroad.

Adjusting to a new country takes time. Start with a few simple steps that can help your new city feel more familiar.

DAY 01–02

Find your footing.

Explore your neighborhood, locate your university, learn nearby public transit routes, and save essential addresses.

DAY 03–04

Build your routine.

Find your favorite grocery store, discover local cafes, understand payment options, and practice everyday phrases.

DAY 05–07

Start exploring.

Connect with classmates, visit somewhere new, and plan your first weekend adventure once you're comfortable with the essentials.

More than a semester

Make somewhere unfamiliar feel like yours.

Studying abroad is an opportunity to grow beyond your comfort zone. There will be wrong turns, unfamiliar words, unexpected changes, and moments when everything feels new. That's part of the experience.

Technology should make navigating those moments easier, not replace the joy of discovering things for yourself. Use these resources as a starting point, then put your phone away, explore your neighborhood, and experience the culture around you.
