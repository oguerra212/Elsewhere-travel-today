How to Travel Europe While Studying Abroad

From spontaneous weekend getaways to carefully planned adventures, discover how to explore Europe without missing classes, overspending, or getting lost along the way.

The ELSEWHERE travel guide

See more of Europe. Stress less along the way.

Studying abroad offers a unique opportunity to experience different cities, cultures, and ways of life. But traveling between countries can feel intimidating when you are unfamiliar with transportation systems, accommodation options, and travel costs.

The key is to plan around your academic schedule, compare your options, and leave room for unexpected discoveries. You do not need an unlimited budget to create unforgettable memories.

Travel smarter

Six steps to planning your next European adventure

01. Plan around your classes

Check your syllabus before booking anything. Use long weekends, academic breaks, and free days to explore nearby cities without interfering with coursework. Keep important deadlines and attendance requirements in mind.

02. Compare trains, buses, and flights

Use Omio and Trainline to research rail and bus routes, and Skyscanner to compare flights. Budget airlines such as Ryanair may offer low advertised fares, but always check baggage allowances, airport locations, and additional fees.

03. Book accommodation strategically

Hostels and shared accommodations can help reduce costs. Compare total prices, read recent reviews, and consider proximity to public transportation. Check cancellation policies and whether the property has secure storage for belongings.

04. Set a realistic travel budget

Account for transportation, accommodation, meals, attractions, local transit, and unexpected expenses. Splitwise can help organize shared costs with friends, while Wise may be useful for managing money across currencies.

05. Prepare before you leave

Download offline maps, save booking confirmations, and learn basic phrases in the local language. Check entry requirements for each destination, including passport validity, visa or residence-permit rules, and any applicable travel restrictions.

06. Leave time to experience the destination

Rather than filling every hour with sightseeing, make time for local cafés, neighborhoods, markets, and conversations. Some of the best travel memories happen when you slow down and explore beyond the main attractions.

Travel inspiration

Make the most of a weekend abroad.

A weekend getaway does not need to involve flying across Europe. Start by exploring destinations a short train ride from your study-abroad city. Nearby trips can be more affordable, easier to organize, and just as memorable.

Before booking, compare the total journey time rather than the ticket price alone. A slightly more expensive train departing from the city center may be more convenient than an inexpensive flight from a distant airport.

Above all, remember that studying abroad is not a competition to visit the most countries. It is an opportunity to experience new places with curiosity and intention.
